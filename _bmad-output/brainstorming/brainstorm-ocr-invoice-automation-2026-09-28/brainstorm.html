PACKING SLIP · WHAT CAME IN THE BOX

A sport-shoe chain, buried in paper

A retail sport-shoe chain gets thousands of supplier material invoices every month. Many of them arrive as phone photos shared on WhatsApp. Someone reads each one, keys the fields by hand, and sends them on to the existing accounts system.

Goal: pull the invoice data out automatically, push it into the accounts system, and add analytics that show which vendors ship slower.

WhatsApp photo→human eyes→hand keying→accounts system

Set-up: the coach worked as a Creative Partner and picked two techniques. First Assumption Reversal, then Morphological Analysis.

Assumption Reversal

Any invoice could be fake

  • YouReverse “the invoice is genuine”: assume any invoice could be fake and build fraud and authenticity checks into intake.
  • CoachZero-trust intake: accept an invoice only if it matches a real PO and a goods-received record. The photo proves nothing on its own.

ASSUMEDYou spot a late shipment when it arrives.
FLIPPED

The missing invoice is the signal

  • YouThe PO has an expected date (PO on 1 Jan, expected 7 Jan). If no invoice photo turns up until much later, a missing invoice past its expected date probably means a late delivery.

  • YouThe overdue list automatically sends the supplier a reminder through the supplier upload app.

Two detours that ended in roadblocks

Let the supplier fix it?

  • CoachThe upload page shows the extracted fields back to the supplier straight away, so they can fix errors such as a wrong supplier ID before submitting.
  • YouSupplier self-correction is a risk: suppliers could lie when they ‘fix’ the extracted data.
  • CoachKeep the original OCR read and the supplier's edits side by side. Any supplier change to amounts, bank details or quantity gets flagged for review, and cosmetic fixes pass.
Rejected: suppliers can't edit. The system trusts what it reads from the photo.

Which date do we believe?

  • YouThe invoice should carry a delivery date.
  • YouCompare the date the photo was taken with the delivery date. If they don't match, send a message.
  • CoachUse the server-side upload timestamp as the trusted ‘photo taken’ time, not only the photo's EXIF data, which can be stripped or faked.
  • CoachThree-date truth for vendor speed: the promised date (PO) vs the delivery date on the invoice vs the goods-received date from our own warehouse.
Correction: a date-mismatch message goes to an admin review queue, not to the supplier or the user.
Rejected: upload timestamp over EXIF. Trust the photo date.
Keep it simple.

  • CoachA supplier scorecard that combines price and speed for each material, to show who's cheapest and who's fastest.

ACT II

Morphological Analysis

Break the system into its parameters, one shelf each, and stock every shelf with options. Five shelves: capture, extraction, validation, integration, analytics and alerts.

Normally you pick one option per row and trace a single path through the matrix. This session didn't. You chose to build every option on every shelf, with no minimal-first slice, minus the logo idea that got dropped.

WHAT MAKES A SUPPLIER ‘BAD’: YOUR THREE TRIPWIRES
  • YouSeveral price increases within a year.
  • YouSlow service (late deliveries).
  • YouA big price gap against other suppliers for the same material.

WATCHLIST

  • YouA bad supplier score triggers a conversation with that supplier.
